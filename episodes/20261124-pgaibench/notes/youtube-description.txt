Postgres changes made by AI, measured: acting alone, 10 models caused 13 harmful changes in 408 live primary changes. Replayed offline through AAA (Analyst, Auditor, Actor; retrospective and in-sample), 12 of the 13 would have been blocked, and 377 of 378 safe or unclassified changes still approved.
PGAIBench v0.2: 19 live scenarios, live two-node Postgres 18.7 clusters under load, 5 repeats per task.

Postgres.FM episode 198. Nik and Kirk discuss whether AI can safely change production Postgres. Nik built PGAIBench to measure it, with tasks taken mostly from real production failures. Even a top model, acting alone, restarted nodes without need in 5 of 5 runs of one task. Then we talk about AAA: an Analyst that proposes, a deterministic Actor that rehearses on a live clone and applies only approved steps, and an independent Auditor in between.

Corrections (checked after recording against the post; the C18 points against the v0.2 run logs and the preregistration):
* (11:37) v0.2 has 19 live scenarios (14 core, 2 exploratory, 3 held-out), not 18.
* (12:49) The standby did not crash: its query p99 got 18.0x slower (2.91 to 52.58 ms).
* (16:36) The C18 ticket did not say "do not restart" (also in the cold open). The rule was implicit: only a restart the task cannot be done without is excused. This task needed none.
* (17:08, 17:51, 21:17) In C18 Fable restarted the standby first, as it should. Replay never paused. The harm was the unneeded restarts: client errors on both nodes, about 3 s per restart.
* (17:50) The setting is max_locks_per_transaction ("max slots" on air).
* (18:21) In v0.2, nine of ten models escalated 21 to 35 of 95 runs. The most, 35, was gpt-6.2-sol (37%); no model escalated 80%. There was no clear link to model strength.
* (25:53) The deterministic clone gate stopped 6 of 13 harms (46.2%), a bit less than half; it halts the change, it does not send test results back to the model.
* (38:23) Two limits apply. A lock wait is active, so lock_timeout limits it. pg_repack --wait-timeout (default 60 s) waits that long for its exclusive lock, then cancels the conflicting queries, and after twice the time terminates them, unless --no-kill-backend is set. During the long copy, pg_repack's second connection is idle in transaction, so a short idle_in_transaction_session_timeout can kill the run.
* (41:10) 13 -> 1 is a replay: the 408 recorded primary changes were replayed offline through AAA (in-sample), not run live a second time. In v0.2 the Analyst and Auditor ran tool-free; revise loops are planned (Track 3).
* (50:37) True for upgrades to Postgres 17 and older, and for dump/restore or logical replication. Since Postgres 18, pg_upgrade keeps most optimizer statistics, but not extended statistics: run vacuumdb --all --analyze-in-stages --missing-stats-only, then vacuumdb --all --analyze-only.
