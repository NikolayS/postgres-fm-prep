Postgres changes made by AI, measured: acting alone, 10 models caused 13 harmful changes in 408 live primary changes. Replayed offline through AAA (Analyst, Auditor, Actor; retrospective and in-sample), 12 of the 13 would have been blocked, and 377 of 378 safe or unclassified changes still approved.
PGAIBench v0.2: 19 live scenarios, live two-node Postgres 18.7 clusters under load, 5 repeats per task.

Postgres.FM episode 198. Nik and Kirk discuss whether AI can safely change production Postgres. Nik built PGAIBench to measure it, with tasks taken mostly from real production failures. Acting alone, even a top model restarted nodes without need in 5 of 5 runs of one task. Then AAA: an Analyst proposes, an independent Auditor reviews, and a deterministic Actor rehearses on a live clone and applies only approved steps.

Corrections (checked after recording against the post, the v0.2 run logs and the preregistration; full text in the show notes):
* (10:55) v0.2 has 19 live scenarios (14 core, 2 exploratory, 3 held-out), not 18.
* (12:07) The standby did not crash: its query p99 got 18.0x slower (2.91 to 52.58 ms).
* (15:54) The C18 ticket did not say "do not restart" (also in the cold open); the rule was implicit.
* (16:26, 17:09, 20:35) Fable restarted the standby first, and replay never paused. The harm was the unneeded restarts.
* (17:39) Nine of ten models escalated 21 to 35 of 95 runs; none came near 80%.
* (25:11) The clone gate stopped 6 of 13 harms (46.2%); it does not send test results back to the model.
* (37:41) pg_repack: lock_timeout limits the lock wait; --wait-timeout cancels, then terminates the conflicting queries.
* (40:28) 13 to 1 is an offline, in-sample replay of the 408 recorded changes through AAA, not a second live run.
* (49:55) Since Postgres 18, pg_upgrade keeps most optimizer statistics (not extended statistics).
