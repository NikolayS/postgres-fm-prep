Postgres changes made by AI, measured: acting alone, 10 models caused 13 harmful changes in 408 live primary changes. Replayed offline through AAA (Analyst, Auditor, Actor; retrospective and in-sample), 12 of the 13 would have been blocked, and 377 of 378 safe or unclassified changes still approved.
PGAIBench v0.2: 19 live scenarios, live two-node Postgres 18.7 clusters under load, 5 repeats per task.

Postgres.FM episode 198. Nik and Kirk discuss whether AI can safely change production Postgres. Nik built PGAIBench to measure it, with tasks taken mostly from real production failures. Even a top model, acting alone, restarted nodes against an explicit rule in 5 of 5 runs. Then we talk about AAA: an Analyst that proposes, a deterministic Actor that rehearses on a live clone and applies only approved steps, and an independent Auditor in between.

Corrections (checked against the post after recording):
* (10:55) v0.2 has 19 live scenarios (14 core, 2 exploratory, 3 held-out), not 18.
* (12:07) The standby did not crash: its query p99 got 18.0x slower (2.91 to 52.58 ms).
* (17:08) The setting is max_locks_per_transaction ("max slots" on air).
* (17:40) No model escalated 80%: gpt-6.2-sol escalated 35 of 95 runs to a human (37%).
* (25:11) The deterministic clone gate stopped 6 of 13 harms (46.2%), a bit less than half; it halts the change, it does not send test results back to the model.
* (37:41) A session that waits for a lock is active, not idle in transaction: limit lock waits with lock_timeout (or pg_repack --wait-timeout).
* (40:28) 13 -> 1 is a replay: the 408 recorded primary changes were replayed offline through AAA (in-sample), not run live a second time. In v0.2 the Analyst and Auditor ran tool-free; revise loops are planned (Track 3).
